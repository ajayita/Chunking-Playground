Chunking Playground

Paste a document, change the chunk size and overlap, ask a question, and see which chunks get retrieved and why.

How to use this & about

The idea

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) apps don't feed a whole document to an AI. They cut it into small pieces called chunks, find the chunks most relevant to a question, and hand only those over. How you cut changes what gets found. This page lets you change the cut and watch the effect.

How to use it

  1. Pick a document. A sample about honeybees is loaded. Paste your own over it any time.
  2. Move the sliders. Watch the colours in step 2 shift to show where each chunk starts and ends.
  3. Ask a question. Type one, or click a sample question.
  4. Compare the two columns. Each shows the top 3 chunks, found two different ways, with a score for each.

Look for the ? buttons: each one explains the thing next to it.

Things to try

  • Set chunk size to 50 and ask a question. The answer gets split across chunks, so no single chunk holds it.
  • Now raise the overlap. Neighbouring chunks share text, so a sentence cut at one edge appears whole in the next chunk.
  • Ask "How do bees cool the hive?" The text says "beat their wings" and "evaporation", not "cool". Compare how the two columns cope.

About

Everything runs in your browser: no server, no account, no API key. Your text never leaves your device. The one download is a small embedding model (about 23 MB, from Hugging Face), fetched on your first visit and cached after that.

This tool only retrieves chunks. It deliberately doesn't write an answer from them, so no language model is involved.

1. Your document

How long each chunk is. Small chunks are precise, but they cut ideas into fragments and lose context. Large chunks keep context, but one chunk then mixes several topics, which blurs matching. The embedding model used here also ignores everything past roughly 1,000 characters of a chunk, so very large chunks are partly invisible to it.

How much text each chunk repeats from the end of the previous one. Without overlap, a sentence that straddles a boundary is split in two and neither half makes full sense. With overlap, it appears whole in at least one chunk. The cost: more chunks, and near-duplicate results. It can't reach the chunk size.

2. Where the chunks fall

This is your document, cut up. Chunks alternate between two colours so you can see where one ends and the next begins. Where two chunks share text (the overlap), that text is shown in a third colour. After you search, chunks that were retrieved get a blue outline. Chunks are cut at an exact character count, so a cut can land mid-word. That is how simple fixed-size chunking behaves.

3. Ask a question

Your question is compared against every chunk in two different ways, and each way returns its 3 best chunks. Change the sliders after searching and the results update, so you can see how chunking changes what is found.

Keyword search (BM25)

Keyword search matches exact words: it finds chunks containing the same words as your question, and has no idea that different words can mean the same thing.

Score: BM25 adds up points for each question word found in a chunk. Rare words earn more than common ones, repeats help with diminishing returns, and shorter chunks get a small boost. There's no maximum, so only compare scores within one search; the bar is relative to the top result.
Highlighted words are the exact matches that earned the score. Words like "the" and "of" are ignored, and "bee" does not match "bees" because there is no word-stem handling. No highlights means no match.

Meaning search (embeddings)

Embeddings match meaning: your question and every chunk become lists of numbers, and the closest ones win, even when they share no words at all.

Score: cosine similarity between the question's numbers and the chunk's numbers, from about 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same meaning). The bar shows the score on that scale. Exact values matter less than the ranking.
No highlighted words, because the match isn't about words. It may pick a chunk that shares none of your words, which keyword search can never do. It can also pick something merely on the same topic, so check the text.

Runs entirely in your browser. No server, no API key. Retrieval only: nothing here generates answers.
