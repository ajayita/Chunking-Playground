Chunking playground

Paste a document, choose a chunk size and overlap, and see which pieces a question retrieves, and why.

Note

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you paste or upload leaves your device. This tool only retrieves chunks; it doesn't write answers.

Document

Paste text, or upload a PDF, DOCX, ODT, TXT or Markdown file. Files are read in your browser and are never uploaded.

Characters per chunk, from 50 to 2,000.

How long each chunk is. Small chunks are precise, but they cut ideas into fragments and lose context. Large chunks keep context, but one chunk then mixes several topics, which blurs matching. The embedding model used here also ignores everything past roughly 1,000 characters of a chunk, so very large chunks are partly invisible to it.

Characters shared between neighbouring chunks.

How much text each chunk repeats from the end of the previous one. Without overlap, a sentence that straddles a boundary is split in two and neither half makes full sense. With overlap, it appears whole in at least one chunk. The cost: more chunks, and near-duplicate results. Overlap can't reach the chunk size.

Ask a question

Your question is compared against every chunk in two different ways, and each way returns its 3 best chunks. Change the sliders after searching and the results update, so you can see how chunking changes what is found.

The sample questions only fit the sample document. Ask your own question about your text.

Keyword search

BM25

Keyword search matches exact words: it finds chunks containing the same words as your question, and has no idea that different words can mean the same thing.

Score: BM25 adds up points for each question word found in a chunk. Rare words earn more than common ones, repeats help with diminishing returns, and shorter chunks get a small boost. There's no maximum, so only compare scores within one search; the bar is relative to the top result.
Highlighted words are the exact matches that earned the score. Words like "the" and "of" are ignored, and "bee" does not match "bees" because there is no word-stem handling. No highlights means no match.

Meaning search

Embeddings

Embeddings match meaning: your question and every chunk become lists of numbers, and the closest ones win, even when they share no words at all.

Score: cosine similarity between the question's numbers and the chunk's numbers, from about 0 (unrelated) to 1 (same meaning). The bar shows the score on that scale. Exact values matter less than the ranking.
No highlighted words, because the match isn't about words. It may pick a chunk that shares none of your words, which keyword search can never do. It can also pick something merely on the same topic, so check the text.

Guide

The idea

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) apps don't feed a whole document to an AI. They cut it into small pieces called chunks, find the chunks most relevant to a question, and hand only those over. How you cut changes what gets found. This page lets you change the cut and watch the effect.

How to use it

  1. Pick a document. A sample about honeybees is loaded. Paste your own text over it, or upload a PDF, DOCX, ODT, TXT or Markdown file.
  2. Move the sliders. Watch the shading in the chunk map shift to show where each chunk starts and ends.
  3. Ask a question. Type one, or choose a sample question.
  4. Compare the two columns. Each shows the top 3 chunks, found two different ways, with a score for each.

Each What does this do? button explains the control above it.

Things to try

  • Set chunk size to 50 and ask a question. The answer gets split across chunks, so no single chunk holds it.
  • Now raise the overlap. Neighbouring chunks share text, so a sentence cut at one edge appears whole in the next chunk.
  • With the sample document, ask "How do bees cool the hive?" The text says "beat their wings" and "evaporation", not "cool". Compare how the two columns cope.

What it does

  • Paste any text. A sample document is preloaded so it works straight away.
  • Upload a file: PDF, DOCX, ODT, TXT or Markdown, or drop it onto the text box. The text is extracted in your browser, and you can edit it afterwards.
  • Chunk size slider (50 to 2,000 characters).
  • Overlap slider, in characters.
  • Chunk map: your document shaded by chunk, with overlaps in a third shade.
  • Chunk stats: total chunks and average size.
  • Ask a question and get the top 3 chunks.
  • Two retrieval modes side by side: keyword (BM25) and meaning (embeddings).
  • Scores on every result, with a bar to compare at a glance.
  • Matched words highlighted in keyword results.
  • Retrieved chunks outlined in the chunk map.
  • Live updates: results refresh as you move the sliders.
  • Plain-English notes under each mode, and an explanation behind every control.
  • Loading indicator while the model downloads on your first visit.
  • Private: runs entirely in your browser, with no server or API key.
  • Works on desktop and mobile, in light or dark mode.

What it doesn't do

  • Generate answers. It only retrieves chunks, so no language model is involved.
  • Read old-style .doc files (save as .docx first) or scanned PDFs, which are just images with no text to extract.

About

Chunking playground is a teaching tool for people learning RAG who have only seen chunking in neat tutorials. It shows the step most frameworks hide behind a default setting, and explains every result in plain language.

Everything runs in your browser: no server, no account, no API key. Your text never leaves your device. The one download is a small embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, about 23 MB, from Hugging Face), fetched on your first visit and cached after that.

Keyword search, chunking and similarity scoring are written by hand in plain JavaScript. Only the embedding model comes from a library (Transformers.js).
